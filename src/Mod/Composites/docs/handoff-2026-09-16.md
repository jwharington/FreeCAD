## 3. Cross-validation — variant (b) run; material-export defect fixed

Variant (b) (draped per-ply export, user-authorised) was solved on the
**same** flat plate and mesh as variant (a). After the force-unit and
result-mapping bugs it still disagreed by **~27%** (mean axial ux) — a
real, mesh-converged difference, not the old 32.8% measurement artefact.

A controlled experiment (per-element frame forced to a uniform identity
frame at runtime — no file changed, nextdrape untouched) isolated the
cause:

1. **Per-ply material export — root cause, fixed `1aa33763fe`.**
   `merge_single` baked each ply's rotation into engineering constants
   and `write_lamina_material_ccx` emitted `TYPE=ENGINEERING CONSTANTS`.
   A rotated ply is *monoclinic*: 9 orthotropic constants cannot carry the
   normal-shear coupling, so the rotated stiffness was silently replaced
   by a different (orthotropic) tensor — `Q̄11(45)`: 28404 → 15649,
   `Q̄66(45)`: 20419 → 9654, giving `A11` 58414 → 48210 MPa·mm (−18%) and
   an ~11% too-compliant laminate. Fix: retain the full rotated 6×6
   stiffness and emit `*ELASTIC,TYPE=ANISOTROPIC` (verified against
   CalculiX `test/aniso.inp` and `umat.f`; component order permuted).
2. **Backend mesh-edge frame — NOT a defect (rework reverted `00c2d5c7ec`).**
   A QI stack is rotation-invariant in-plane, so any per-element frame
   gives the same membrane answer.  A rework to derive warp/weft from
   the drape field (`_field_frame`) was written, then withdrawn: it
   solved a non-problem and was O(elements × quads) and slow.  An
   earlier apparent frame "effect" was a cross-run mesh difference
   (8045 vs 8049 nodes at clmax 2), not the frame.

**Result (mean axial ux, draped vs QI):**

| clmax | nodes | before material fix | after material fix |
|---|---|---|---|
| 30 | 65 | 30.5% | 0.03% |
| 7.5 | 657 | 26.3% | 0.25% |
| 2.0 | 8045 | 27.6% | 1.59% |

The post-fix residual grows slightly with refinement and is investigated
as the composite-16-layer vs single-layer shell difference
(through-thickness / transverse-shear treatment) and/or draped-mesh
convergence — **not** the per-element frame.

## 8. Orientation: what is NOT established (added later, 2026-09-16)

### 8.1 The reported defect

On the mixed QI stiffener panel the fibre orientation either side of the
stiffener web, and in the foot strip, disagree.  They must not: the foot
strip is the *same physical surface* as the panel, laminated with the
stiffener.

### 8.2 What the evidence shows (measured, headless)

```
PanelShell        support = ZStiffener_RemainderSupport
                  rosette = PanelRosette (30 deg)   LCS X = (0.866, +0.500, 0)
                  support face normal = (0, 0, +1)
                  _frame_seed() warp = +30.00 deg
ZStiffener_Foot   support = ZStiffener_Foot_Support
                  rosette = ZStiffener_PanelFootTransfer (30 deg)
                                 LCS X = (0.866, -0.500, 0)
                  support face normal = (0, 0, -1)
                  _frame_seed() warp = -30.00 deg
```

Key facts:

* the panel is **not one draped region** — `PanelShell` drapes the stiffener
  *remainder*, and the foot strip is a **separate shell draped under a
  different rosette**;
* the rosette angle **is** the nextdrape seed: `_frame_seed()` returns the
  LCS X-axis as the seed's `warp_direction` (pre-existing code, unchanged);
* the foot's support face normal is **opposite** the panel's, and
  `TransferRosette._axis_angle` measures each frame's warp about *that
  frame's own Z*, so the same world fibre line reads −30° on the foot
  against +30° on the panel;
* the transfer solve drives *that* residual to zero, so it converges to the
  **mirrored** fibre, and the foot is then seeded 60° away from the panel.
* `DrapeValid` is `True` for both shells throughout — which is why every
  pre-existing assertion passed.

### 9.4 The three-face requirement, and the one remaining fault

The stiffener's foot cuts the panel into two disconnected remainder faces;
with the foot strip, the three coplanar faces (remainder A, foot,
remainder B) must all carry the panel's fibre orientation in global
coordinates.  That now holds **except on a strip along the lower
remainder's bottom edge**: of 216 grid samples, 54 are wrong (azimuths
0–17° instead of 30°), all at y ≤ 15, while y ≥ 25 is correct everywhere.

Cause, measured: the remainder's drape develops
`BOUNDARY-LINK-BORN-OFF-EDGE` faults along its long edge (boundary links
born up to ~5–10 mm off the part edge against a 0.5 mm tolerance; 279
traps on the real remainder, 130 on the isolated shape) — the
boundary-snapped nodes and fragment quads there carry corrupted frames,
and the (now working) lookup exposes them.  This is **nextdrape boundary
lattice behaviour, pre-existing** (the traps predate the multi-group
change); the lookup itself is correct and tested.

**Handed over to nextdrape:**
`src/3rdParty/nextdrape/docs/handover-remainder-boundary-frame-fault.md`
with the reproduction, the red test, and the fix scope.  Do not fix it
from the Composites side.

### 11.1 Summary of the design

A dart placed on a composite shell must act as a **cut boundary**:
fabric stops at the wire, the texture plan is cut along it (slit in the
plan), and per-ply `get_boundaries` split there. Today the dart is only a
*blocking band* in the lattice (`cutWires` proximity reject + quad-cross
cut) — the plan splits around band edges offset ~0.5 mm from the wire,
and the wire itself is never boundary geometry.

- **Spanning dart** (wire ends on the part edge): the support face is
  **split** into two faces at the wire — zero solver changes.
- **Interior dart** (tip inside): zero-width slit routed through the
  **existing Phase-2 boundary-snap law** — a placement link crossing the
  registered dart wire is a boundary violation; the candidate is placed
  ON the wire at the crossing point (`MakeBoundaryNode`,
  `NodePlacementKind::BoundarySnapped`). No ε: a "slit as a hairpin hole"
  is invisible to the even-odd `IsInsideFace` test (the hairpin
  traverses the same curve twice), and widening it would be a forbidden
  approximation.
- The `cutWires` proximity-reject band is retired from the drape path.

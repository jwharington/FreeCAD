1. Verdict

Confirmed: web_band is not the failing shape. It is an analytic periodic cylinder (IsUPeriodic()=1), so the baseline10 seam-stop law (RelocateSeedOffSeam + stop-at-seam) engages and it solves 1.0 coverage. The production web compound has different topology on both faces, and both observed failures trace to that difference — plus one genuine solver-side rejection that the periodic cylinder never exercises.

4. Problem A — the web face doesn't cover (0.26, wedge)

Evidence from ND_SOLVE_DEBUG/ND_QUAD_DEBUG runs and the full result JSON:

  • The seed lands on the v=0 domain edge of face0 (node (0,0): uv = (4.712, 0.000000) at r=100, z=60). On a periodic face, RelocateSeedOffSeam would move it off the seam; here the periodicity flag is absent, so no relocation.
  • The plain weft advance off that edge fails immediately: SolveAdvancePoint's first metric candidate lands at v<0 — outside the domain — and is rejected before any Newton iteration or border-clamp path (1067 events, sources spread over the whole u range).
  • 1311 further nodes are accepted at j<0 (u spans 0.006→6.278 — the march did wrap the u-seam, and 183 of them sit at v≈0). These come from the fallback placement routes (atlas / edge transport), not the plain march.
  • But the cells they occupy are rejected by the quad builder: 745 pushed-invalid quads, the canonical pattern being quad (0,-1) corners-valid: 0 0 1 1 — cells straddling the seed edge row and the fallback rows have mixed-valid corners (uv-outside), so no fabric is emitted there. Coverage in nodes ≠ coverage in fabric.
  • The frontier then stagnates (openRemaining=17, massive re_solve churn) and the coverage freezes at ≈ the flange-band fraction.

Residual unknown (A): why the warp wedge terminates at i ∈ [−51, 49] (±73° around the seed) — nothing geometrically special sits there. The stagnation threshold (noGrowthRoundsThreshold = 8) is the likely freezer once both weft directions block, but the trigger for the warp columns themselves was not pinned in this pass.

5. Problem B — no wrap around the edge onto the flange

The crossing machinery does find the shared edge (r=110, z=60) between face0 and face1: SEAM-MARCH logs show exit edge + crossing point + arc split (arcA=0.54 mm, arcB=1.96 mm) — the trace, adjacency and chirality stages all pass. The failure is uniformly at the landing solve:

SEAM-MARCH cell(27,0) src=(26,0) crossing=(64.37,-89.20,60)
    arcA=0.539 arcB=1.961  rejected by landing solve

1067 of 1067 crossing attempts rejected this way. The landing step runs SolveAdvancePoint on face1 from the crossing point (which sits on face1's v=0 edge) with the kinked direction; its initial metric candidate lands at v < 0 — outside face1's domain — and is rejected outright (strict uvSlack = tol, added in the baseline10 seam fix). With no landing point the fold law has nothing to weld, crossFace = 0, and face1 stays empty.

Residual unknown (B): whether the landing direction genuinely points off face1 (kink/chirality sign error on this trimmed-cylinder pair) or whether the initial candidate construction is degenerate when the source sits exactly on the domain edge. One targeted instrumented pass inside ApplySeamStep would discriminate — not done here (solver untouched, per instruction).

6. The shared structural root cause

Both failures share one trigger: the swept web/flange band is geometrically self-closed but carries no periodicity flag (IsUPeriodic()=0, IsUClosed()=1, full 2π domain, u-edge gap ~1e-13 mm). Everything the solver does for a self-connected surface — seed relocation off the seam, wrap of the advance across the parameter seam, stop-at-seam classification — is keyed on the flag, so on this face the machinery behaves as if the band were an open patch with two coincident boundary edges:

  • the seed is not relocated off the boundary edge → weft starts from the edge;
  • off-edge/off-domain advances fall through to fallback placement, which lands edge-sliding/wrapped nodes whose cells the quad builder rejects;
  • crossings onto the neighbour face die at the landing solve's strict domain test (this part would need a genuine solver-side fix regardless of flags).

This matches the handoff's follow-up guidance: detect self-connection geometrically (domain-edge coincidence — here 1e-13 mm — or a seed round-trip probe), not by surface type. The C++-side probe would see exactly what drape_cli --shapefile now prints.

7. Open sub-questions before any fix attempt

  1. Pin the warp-wedge terminus (±51 steps) with one instrumented frontier run.
  2. Decompose the face1 landing rejection: direction geometry (kink sign) vs initial-candidate degeneracy at an on-edge source.
  3. Decide whether the fix is (a) geometric self-connection detection feeding the existing seam law (matches handoff guidance), or (b) a landing-solve boundary-row path for cross-face steps — the evidence suggests both are needed: A is flag-gated, B fails on a genuinely periodic face1 whose landing candidate still goes off-domain.

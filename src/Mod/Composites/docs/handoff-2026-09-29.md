## 0. What this is

A day's work on the composites flow while porting a fuselage to the
workbench.  Three defects were diagnosed to their mechanical root, two of them
fixed and measured; one is a trade-off that is now pinned by tests.  The full
issue entries live in `docs/known-issues.md` (#11, #12, #13); this document is
the state, the evidence, and the next steps.

**Constraints in force (user's, do not violate):**

- **No extrusion.** **No conversion of anything to a solid.**  Primitive OCCT
  operations only.
- Never weaken an assertion or a threshold to make a test pass.
- Hand stiffeners **plain loft** supports (see #11).

### #12 — the seat filter's tool-side fragments (the only blocker for counts)

Remainder piece counts are high: `6` against `2` on the plate fixtures,
`5` against `0` on the ring.  The pieces wanted are present; the extras come
from the stiffener's own faces.  The exact filter is the fuse's provenance
map (`GeneralFuseResult.piecesFromSource`), but turning it on breaks the
multi-stiffener chain with `SeamCompositeLaminateFP: Master shares no boundary
edge with the seam region`.

### #13 — the cone case

`test_t_section_on_a_tilted_cut_of_a_conical_panel` expects 80 and gets 60:
on a tilted cut of a cone OCCT's offset gives up too, and the rebuilt row
differs.  The rebuild needs to reproduce the offset's geometry for a conical
generator (its `b` is not the `t x N` of a planar row).

### Part-side follow-up

`FuselageV2.py`'s `_frame_sleeve` still pre-trims its sleeves with a boolean
`common`.  That is scaffolding only (the airframe is built separately and is
unaffected), but it must be reduced to a **plain loft** spanning the frame
section, or the seat cut silently disappears on that part (#11).  The ported
script also still needs its end-to-end run.

## 4. Things worth knowing before touching this

- **A surface that has been through a Boolean cannot be split or cut by
  anything** in this OCCT build (measured against cut, generalFuse, SplitAPI
  slice, 1-D tools, fuzzy 1e-3/1e-2).  This is the whole of #11.
- **`_shape_of` re-derives the support shape on every call**, so
  `Shape.hashCode()` changes between calls and content fingerprints walk
  vertices in traversal order (a rebuilt compound fingerprints differently for
  identical geometry).  Caches keyed on either miss every time; the
  order-independent `shape_stamp` works.
- **A face cut along a curve on it stays one face** with unchanged area (the
  cut appears as an internal edge) — "same area, one face" is not evidence
  that nothing happened.
- **`Shape.BoundBox` over-reports on these B-spline faces** (it can be the
  whole untrimmed surface's box).  Compare areas, not bounding boxes.
- The module's `_resupport_panel` guard keeps a panel on its own support when
  a seat leaves no remainder; it *hides* the seat-cut defect rather than
  fixing it, and the validation contract around consumed remainders is what
  #12 runs into.

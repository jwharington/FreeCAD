## 2. The seat cut, from defect to contract

The remainder is the support minus the seat, and two rules give it exactly.
They were landed together once the measurement showed what was missing.

### 2.1 Select pieces by provenance, not by centre of mass (`16b15a71ec`)

`_support_remainders` splits each support face by the stiffener's faces and
keeps a piece only when the fuse records that it came from the **support
alone**.  A piece generated by the support *and* a tool is the region they
share — the seat — and is dropped.  No geometry is guessed.

The previous selection took *every* piece the fuse returned, so the
**stiffener's own swept faces** were listed as support remainder — those are
the five stray surfaces in
`/home/jmw/Desktop/stiffener-review/07-ring-remainder-fragments.png` — and
the centre-of-mass filter that was papering over it is deleted.

### 2.2 Pass the fuse only the tools that meet the face (`49ec29c8a2`)

A tool that does not reach a support face cannot split it, and passing it
anyway shortened the fuse's provenance map — OCCT reports *"Map entry 0 is
empty.  Source-to-piece correspondence information is probably incomplete."*
— so `piecesFromSource` returned nothing for the support and the whole
untouched piece vanished from the remainder.

Measured on the chained fixture (two stiffeners, one plate) with
`compositestests/inspect_chain_remainders.py`:

| link | faces | area |
|---|---|---|
| A's support (the plate) | 1 | 7200 |
| A's remainder | 2 | 4800 = 7200 - 2400 (A's seat) ✓ |
| B's SupportBase (= A's remainder) | 2 | 4800 (y[0,30] and y[50,60]) |
| B's remainder **before the fix** | 1 | **1200** ✗ — y[50,60] was lost (B's seat at y[10,30] never reaches it) |
| B's remainder **after the fix** | 2 | **2400** = 4800 - 2400 ✓ |

The lost half-plate was why every seam in the chain failed its shared-edge
check: the panel's weave fell short of the plate it was supposed to cover, so
the ten `TestMultipleStiffenersOnOnePanel` failures were the *consequence*,
not a validation contract to relax.

### 2.3 What the rings do

A ring's sleeve *is* the frame section, so its support has no surface beyond
the seat and its **remainder is empty** — the geometry's own answer, which is
what `test_seat_cut_is_justified_by_the_geometry` asserts (empty, *and* the
area identity that justifies it).

## 5. Things worth knowing before touching this

- **A surface that has been through a Boolean cannot be split or cut by
  anything** in this OCCT build — measured against `cut`, `generalFuse`,
  `SplitAPI.slice`, 1-D tools, and `fuzzy_value` 1e-3/1e-2. This is all of
  #11: hand over a plain loft.
- **`_shape_of` re-derives the support shape on every call**, so
  `Shape.hashCode()` changes between calls, and content fingerprints walk
  vertices in traversal order (a rebuilt compound fingerprints differently for
  identical geometry). Caches keyed on either miss every time; the
  order-independent `shape_stamp` works.
- **A face cut along a curve on it stays one face** with unchanged area (the
  cut appears as an internal edge) — "one face, same area" is not evidence
  that nothing happened.
- **`Shape.BoundBox` over-reports on these B-spline faces** (it can be the
  whole untrimmed surface's box). Compare areas, not bounding boxes.
- `_resupport_panel` keeps a panel on its own support when a seat leaves no
  remainder (the ring case).  That is correct, not a hiding place: the seam's
  shared-edge check finds the foot inside the whole support because it accepts
  an edge of one shape lying on the other.
- **A partial provenance map loses pieces silently.**  `generalFuse` can
  return a face its tools never touched with no recorded source, and
  `piecesFromSource` then drops it.  Ask the fuse only to split where a tool
  reaches; do not read an empty piece list as "nothing is left".
- **The runner's PASS log can be corrupted by OCCT carriage-return spam**, so
  a test can run and pass without its `PASS` line surviving `grep '^PASS'`.
  The timings file the runner writes from Python (`FREECAD_TEST_TIMINGS`, path
  printed at the top of the run) is the authoritative per-test record — read
  it before concluding a test did not run.
